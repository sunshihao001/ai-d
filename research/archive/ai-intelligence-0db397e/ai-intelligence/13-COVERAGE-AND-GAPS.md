# 13｜Coverage and Research Gaps

## Purpose

Research should not only ask:

> What did we find?

It must also ask:

> What important parts have we not investigated?

Coverage is the mechanism that turns research into an adaptive loop.

## Coverage model

A research goal may be decomposed into meaningful dimensions.

Example:

`Skill Discovery / Generation / Evaluation / Versioning / Regression / Feedback`

Each dimension can be:

- COVERED
- PARTIAL
- MISSING
- CONTRADICTED
- UNKNOWN
- NOT_APPLICABLE

Coverage is about decision-relevant dimensions, not arbitrary taxonomy.

## Gap

A gap is an unresolved question or missing evidence that could materially change the current conclusion.

Each gap should record:

- gap_id
- research_goal
- dimension
- current understanding
- missing evidence
- importance
- candidate search directions
- blocking decision, if any
- status

## Gap-directed research

When a gap is material:

`Gap → Search Task → Evidence → Coverage Update`

The next search should target the gap rather than repeat broad discovery.

## Coverage completion

Do not require 100% coverage.

Research may stop when:

- important dimensions are sufficiently covered;
- remaining gaps are low impact;
- contradictory evidence has been addressed;
- further search is unlikely to change the decision;
- cost exceeds expected information value.

## Discovery feedback

Research findings may create new dimensions.

Therefore coverage is dynamic:

`Current Model → New Evidence → New Dimension/Gap → Follow-up Search`

Coverage must never become a rigid permanent taxonomy.

## Batch integration

Bulk Intake produces candidates.

Coverage determines which candidates deserve deeper research and which new searches should be generated.

## Quality rule

A large number of sources does not imply good coverage.

One highly important missing dimension can matter more than hundreds of redundant sources.
